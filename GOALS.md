# Goals

**Project:** Penny For Your Stocks
**Owner:** Mark Heydon
**Last updated:** 2026-10-29

---

## Why This Exists

Simply hoping that shares will go up is not a viable investment strategy. This project explores whether market data, simple rules and cautious analysis can help identify modest, low-outlay share-dealing opportunities.

It is a hobby and learning project. The curiosity is whether a couple of hundred pounds a month of extra income is achievable from low-risk (in terms of outlay) share dealing. It is not a belief that it will make anyone rich. Like the National Lottery Generator, it is semi lighthearted but properly built, and it is not a novelty app. The realistic chance of anyone making a good sum from it is close to zero, and the project says so plainly.

---

## Goals

- Build an experimental stock analysis and trading application that identifies candidate trades using market data, rules and logic, without overstating certainty.
- Find out, using evidence, whether a strategy can make anything after spreads, fees and costs.
- Practise building an AI-assisted app with Microsoft Foundry.
- Keep the experiment small: a ring-fenced pot of money, real shares only, and no leverage, CFDs or spread betting.
- Keep strategy logic independent of any single broker, so brokers (for example Trading 212, Interactive Brokers) and a paper-trading broker are interchangeable adapters.
- Prove ideas with paper trading before risking real money, and keep a human approving trades until there is evidence that automation is warranted.

---

## Success Looks Like

- The application can analyse market data and present potential opportunities with appropriate scepticism.
- Paper-trading results show, net of costs, whether the strategy has any edge at all. A clear "no" is a valid outcome.
- Microsoft Foundry has been used meaningfully in the app, and the lessons learned are captured.
- Any income target (around £200 a month) is treated as a hope to test, never as a budget or a reason to increase stakes after a bad month.

---

## Kill Criteria

- The project becomes too complicated or takes up too much time. It is a hobby and learning exercise, and there is no time to develop it full time.
- Paper trading shows no edge after costs, and there is no credible idea left to test.
- Pursuing it would mean risking money that is needed for essentials, or raising stakes to chase a target.

---

## What This Is NOT For

(See also: SCOPE.md)

- Promising returns or treating market analysis as certainty.
- Financial advice, or a guaranteed or likely income source.
- Leveraged products or high-risk speculation with money that cannot be lost.
