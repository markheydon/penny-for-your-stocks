# Concept: A bounded trial of modest trading income

- **Slug**: modest-trading-income
- **Created**: 2026-10-29
- **Recommended option**: A written trial with a stop rule

## Options

### Option A — Leave it with the existing ISA

- **Sketch**: No new trial and no new tool. The requester keeps using the stocks and shares ISA they already hold for ready-made plans and individual shares. The question of withdrawing about £100–£200 a month from an active, ring-fenced dealing pot is treated as already answered by the figures in the problem: a long-run UK equity rate around 9% a year nominal is a few pounds a year on about £50, and those monthly withdrawals need on the order of £14,000–£27,000 of capital if the pot is not spent. The 12–18 month review never happens, because nothing is started.
- **Appetite**: small
- **Trade-offs**: Avoids extra turnover, stamp duty or foreign-exchange drag, and wide spreads, and matches the project’s own statement that the chance of a good sum is close to zero. It does not produce the personal pass/fail record the goals ask for, so a later top-up or a later abandonment would still be a guess. There is little left to learn about costs; that work is already in the research note.
- **Rabbit holes**: Reopening broker choice, tax, or platform fees, which the requester has already placed outside this assessment. Treating “do nothing” as a prompt to redesign the existing ISA.

### Option B — A written trial with a stop rule

- **Sketch**: The requester runs one ring-fenced trial for about 12 to 18 months and writes it down. Each check (at least weekly; daily only when they choose) records what was considered, what was held, the intended hold of a few days to about a week, and a plain allowance for spread and dealing costs. The pot starts at about £50 of money that can be lost. Real shares only, in the UK, the United States, or Europe, excluding controversial areas such as weapons. No leverage or derivatives. At about 12 months they apply their own rule — stop if it is clearly just losing money — and they review again by about 18 months. Any withdrawal is compared with the £100–£200 hope and written as observed cash, not as a target the trial must hit. Further money is considered only if the short term is not already a clear failure, and the amount is chosen then. The record is a log the requester keeps (a notebook or a spreadsheet). It is not a product, and it does not suggest shares.
- **Appetite**: small
- **Trade-offs**: This is the smallest way to get the recorded stop-or-continue judgement and a personal answer on whether any cash can be withdrawn. It accepts, up front, that a pot near £50 cannot fund £100–£200 a month at ordinary market rates, and that 12–18 months of one person’s trades cannot show a repeatable edge. What it sacrifices is optional future tooling (a separate analysis application or Microsoft Foundry experiments) until this trial has a result, or those ideas can be dropped if the trial is already a clear loss. The main risk is an unread log, or reading a lucky run as skill. Practice-mode fills, where a broker offers them, are a weak stand-in for real spreads and taxes; the log has to write those costs in by hand or the result will look too kind.
- **Rabbit holes**: Turning the log into a screener, a data feed, or a phone app before the first review. Adding capital to “give the strategy a fair chance” before the short-term check. Tightening the hold toward same-day dealing. Inventing a numeric profit target from the £100 figure, which was a way of saying “stop if it is clearly just losing money”, not a budget.

### Option C — A paper-first process tool

- **Sketch**: Build the experimental application the repository goals describe: a small tool that helps the same person run a weekly process of noting candidates, costs, a human yes or no, and a paper result, then judge at 12 and 18 months whether the trial is clearly just losing money. Brokers stay interchangeable later. No automatic orders. The income figure stays a hope the tool is allowed to falsify.
- **Appetite**: medium (uncertain: a journal with a reminder is closer to small; a market-data and rules engine is a hobby measured in weeks only if scope is held hard, and otherwise becomes large)
- **Trade-offs**: Matches the stated end product (“a tool to help manage the process”) and the learning goals. It does not change the capital arithmetic, and published studies of frequent individual traders point to costs pulling results below the market. Software built before the first logged month can absorb the time the kill criteria say is not available. A clear “no edge” remains a valid outcome, but only if the tool is not extended in search of one.
- **Rabbit holes**: Choosing data providers, screeners, and broker adapters; anything that ranks or recommends individual shares; Foundry features that do not help the 12-month judgement; paper trading that ignores stamp duty, foreign-exchange fees, and the spread.

## Recommendation

Option B. The goals that this assessment can actually serve are a recorded stop-or-continue decision at about 12 months (and a review by about 18 months), a comparison of any cash withdrawn with the £100–£200 hope, and a refusal to treat a short run as a durable edge. A log with a pre-written stop rule does that at a small appetite. Option A never creates the record the requester asked for, so the same question would still be open in a year. Option C spends weeks on software before that record exists, and the evidence does not show that software improves the chance of the income. The income hope itself does not clear the bar on the opening pot: about £4 a year at a long-run nominal UK equity rate, against £1,200–£2,400 a year withdrawn. The trial is worthwhile only as a way to confirm or abandon that hope in the requester’s own book, inside money they can lose.

## Out of Scope (for the recommended option)

- Individual share recommendations, screens that rank shares, or a get-rich-quick outcome.
- Leverage, contracts for difference, spread betting, borrowed money, or other heavily speculative strategies.
- Same-day dealing, or a routine that requires a market check every day.
- Choosing a broker, setting platform fees or platform limits, or giving tax advice.
- Treating 12–18 months of one person’s trades as proof of a repeatable edge.
- Building an analysis or trading application, including Microsoft Foundry work, until a later decision says the written trial is worth tooling.
- Adding capital before the short-term review, or sizing a later pot so that £100–£200 a month becomes the plan.
- Penny shares as a requirement. They stay a starting interest only.

## Assumptions to Validate

- A notebook or spreadsheet the requester actually fills in on the weekly check is enough for the 12-month and 18-month judgement. No application is required for that judgement.
- “Clearly just losing money” can be applied to that log in plain language. The £100 figure remains an illustration of that sentence, not a loss budget and not a profit target.
- Costs the research already flags (bid–offer spread, and stamp duty or foreign-exchange fees where they apply) will be written into each line. A practice-mode result that omits them will not be treated as the trial result.
- The opening sum stays about £50 of money that can be lost until a short-term review says the attempt is not already a clear failure.
- The existing stocks and shares ISA continues for money that is not in the ring-fenced trial.
- The requester will not read a profitable stretch inside 12–18 months as evidence of an edge that justifies larger stakes.
