## Users & Demand

- The only demand signal in this repository is one person asking whether active share dealing could, in time, support withdrawing about £100–£200 a month. They would start with an arbitrary amount of about £50 to test the idea, hold for a few days to about a week, check market data at least once a week, and review after 12–18 months. They already use stocks and shares ISAs for ready-made plans and individual shares, and describe themselves as a basic investor who accepts that the income hope may never be reached. — [source: `.specify/assessments/modest-trading-income/intake.md`] (confidence: high; this is a stated want, not observed trading behaviour)
- On 2026-10-29, after this note was first written, the requester corrected three readings. About £50 is a way to start and test the waters, and is not a decision that the pot stays at that size. Any further money depends on the short term: if it is already clear the idea is not working, it stops; if it is not, more money might be added later and the amount would be reviewed then, not fixed now. The £100 figure means that if, after about a year, the idea is clearly just losing money, it stops. It was not meant as a target. Platform fees, platform limits, and tax are out of scope of this assessment discussion. — [source: requester, this session, 2026-10-29] (confidence: high)
- No support tickets, interviews, usage logs, or closed trades were found. The repository describes itself as early-stage scaffolding and not yet a usable trial log application. — [source: `README.md`] (confidence: high)
- The project goals record the same hope (about £100–£200 a month as observation, not a target), a ring-fenced pot, real shares only within stated boundaries, and state that the realistic chance of anyone making a good sum from it is close to zero. The current product direction is a written trial log with stop rule, not an analysis or trading application. — [source: `GOALS.md`] (confidence: high)

## Prior Art

- Nothing in this repository has yet tested a strategy. Scope defines the trial log application and explicit exclusions (no share recommendations, broker execution, or market data feeds). The `adr/` directory has no decision file beyond its README. Goals require a durable personal record, milestone reviews, income treated as a hope, and treating a clear failure as a valid outcome. — [source: `GOALS.md`, `SCOPE.md`, `README.md`] (confidence: high)
- US discount-broker households in 1991–1996 underperformed after costs. Across 66,465 households, the average household earned 16.4% a year net, against a market return of 17.9%. Households that traded most earned 11.4%. Average turnover was 75% of the portfolio a year. Gross returns were close to the market; the shortfall was largely trading costs and turnover. — [source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/0022-1082.00226] (confidence: high for that sample; medium as a guide to a UK commission-free account in 2026, because the market, era, and costs differ)
- A separate study of Taiwanese day traders (1992–2006) found that the vast majority lost money. In an average year about 450,000 individuals day-traded; about 20% of those with more than about US$20,000 of day-trading volume had positive abnormal returns net of fees in that year, which the authors note is partly what luck would produce. Fewer than 1% (about 4,000) went on to earn reliably positive abnormal returns net of fees the following year. Day trading there means buying and selling the same stock on the same day. — [source: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.finmar.2013.05.006] (confidence: high for that sample). The holding period recorded in intake is a few days to about a week, and same-day trading is outside the stated commitment, so this paper is evidence about short-horizon speculation and cost drag, not a direct measurement of the proposed hold. — [source: `intake.md` for the hold; the paper for the definition] (confidence: high)
- An older London Stock Exchange study of AIM found that a minority of AIM stocks traded frequently, while the majority traded infrequently, with trades clustered on a few days, higher volatility, and wider spreads. The underlying transactions are from the 1990s. — [source: https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/119137/1/dp301.pdf] (confidence: medium for the historical sample; low as a description of AIM liquidity in 2026)
- The FCA Handbook defines a "penny share" as a readily realisable security whose bid–offer spread is 10% or more of the offer price, excluding government securities, FTSE 100 shares, and issuers with a market capitalisation of £100 million or more. That is a spread test, not a price-in-pence test. — [source: https://handbook.fca.org.uk/glossary/G846] (confidence: high)
- The FCA's InvestSmart material says high-risk investments can produce lower returns than mainstream investments, that capital can be lost entirely, and that a common rule of thumb is to put no more than 10% of total net assets into high-risk investments. It also warns that sharp unexplained price rises in small shares are a pump-and-dump pattern. The high-risk page describes the FCA's high-risk investment category; the penny-share glossary entry is a different definition, and this research does not establish that every small listed share sits in the marketing-restriction category. — [source: https://www.fca.org.uk/investsmart/understanding-high-risk-investments ; https://www.fca.org.uk/investsmart/pump-and-dump-schemes] (confidence: high for the published warnings; medium for how they map onto a short list of listed shares)

## Market & Context

- The stated alternative already in use is a stocks and shares ISA holding ready-made plans and individual shares. Doing nothing active keeps that approach and avoids extra turnover costs. — [source: `intake.md`] (confidence: high as a description of current behaviour; the results of those existing holdings were not supplied)
- A published summary of Barclays equity-gilt data reports UK equities at 8.86% a year nominal and 4.95% a year real from 1899 to 2024, with inflation averaging 3.91%. The same family of data, in a Barclays note, shows UK equities up 52.5% in real terms over 2004–2024 (a long-run market result, not a one-year expectation). Both sources say past performance is not a forecast. These figures are buy-and-hold market returns. They are not evidence that frequent dealing earns the same. — [source: https://www.courtiers.co.uk/news-and-insights/125-years-of-data-courtiers-equity-and-gilt-study/ ; https://home.barclays/insights/2026/02/The-Missed-Opportunity-Of-Not-Investing/] (confidence: medium; secondary write-ups of the Barclays series, not the original tables)
- Applying that 8.86% nominal figure as arithmetic only: £50 × 0.0886 is about £4.43 a year, roughly 37 pence a month. A £1,200-a-year withdrawal (£100 a month) at 8.86% would require about £13,540 of capital. A £2,400-a-year withdrawal (£200 a month) would require about £27,090. At the 4.95% real figure the same withdrawals imply about £24,240 and £48,480. None of these rates is a forecast for this account. — [source: arithmetic on the Courtiers/Barclays snippet and the income range in `intake.md`] (confidence: high for the arithmetic; medium for the historical rate used as the input)
- On a £50 starting pot, £100 a month is £1,200 a year, which is 24 times the pot (2,400% a year). £200 a month is 48 times the pot (4,800% a year). The first £100 withdrawn is already twice the starting cash. Withdrawing the gain spends it, so the pot does not compound unless later gains replace what was taken. — [source: arithmetic on `intake.md`] (confidence: high)

That is one advertised rival price, not a survey of the market. — [source: https://www.trading212.com/isa ; arithmetic] (confidence: medium)

## Data & Constraints

The £1,000 threshold applies to paper Stamp Duty on a stock transfer form. The 0.5% charge applies to existing shares in a UK company and to shares in a foreign company with a UK share register. New issues bought from the company, gifts, and units bought from an OEIC or unit-trust manager are outside the usual charge. — [source: https://www.gov.uk/tax-buy-shares/buy-shares-electronically ; https://www.gov.uk/tax-buy-shares ; https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/stamp-duty-and-stamp-duty-reserve-tax/stamp-duty-and-stamp-duty-reserve-tax] (confidence: high)

Trading 212's help text repeats that most LSE AIM stocks are exempt. A separate UK Listing Relief exempts transfers for three years after a company's first listing on a UK regulated market, for companies first listed on or after 27 November 2025. Neither exemption removes the bid–offer spread. — [source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/stamp-taxes-shares-manual/stsm041270 ; https://www.gov.uk/guidance/stamp-duty-reserve-tax-reliefs-and-exemptions ; https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/stamp-taxes-shares-manual/stsm042600 ; Trading 212 fees article above] (confidence: high)

- The opening amount of about £50 and the £100 stop-line were quantified in intake, then clarified on 2026-10-29. £50 is an arbitrary start, not a permanent pot. - The published persistence test in the Taiwan day-trading paper used a subsequent year and a population of hundreds of thousands. One account, checked weekly, over 12–18 months, does not supply that sample. A full weekly round trip would be on the order of 50 trades a year; fewer trades if the pot is not turned over every week. — [source: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.finmar.2013.05.006 ; arithmetic on `intake.md`] (confidence: high that the published test is much larger than this experiment; the mapping from "a few days to a week" to 50 trades is an assumption about turnover)

## Evidence Against the Idea

- A pot that stayed at about £50 cannot fund withdrawals of £100–£200 a month unless that cash is replaced at 2,400–4,800% a year. A historical UK equity market return around 9% nominal would produce a few pounds a year on £50, and on the order of £14,000–£27,000 would be required to fund those withdrawals without spending capital. The requester has said £50 is only an arbitrary start, that any later top-up is conditional on the short term and is not set now, and that the monthly range is a hope rather than a return the opening pot is expected to produce. — [source: arithmetic above; Courtiers summary of Barclays 1899–2024; `intake.md`; requester, this session, 2026-10-29] (confidence: high for the arithmetic on a £50 pot; high that the requester does not treat £50 as the lasting pot)
- Published studies of individual investors find that trading costs, not a lack of gross opportunity alone, are what pull results below the market, and that only a very small share of short-horizon speculators show profits that persist after costs. The samples are not this market and, in the day-trading paper, not this holding period. They are still the strongest external performance evidence found. — [source: Barber and Odean, Journal of Finance, 2000; Barber, Lee, Liu and Odean, Journal of Financial Markets, 2014] (confidence: medium as applied to this idea)
- A weekly recycle of a fully invested £50 pot spends a large fraction of the pot on stamp duty or FX before spreads. A Handbook penny-share spread of 10% or more can remove about a tenth of a £50 position on entry. AIM's stamp-duty exemption removes one tax and leaves the liquidity and spread problem that the older AIM study described. — [source: GOV.UK SDRT rate; Trading 212 FX fee; FCA penny-share glossary; HMRC growth-market exemption; LSE AIM discussion paper] (confidence: medium)
- A commitment to look at least weekly, with holds of a few days to a week, means a position can run through its intended life between looks. Twelve to eighteen months of one person's trades cannot reproduce the statistical separation of luck and persistence used in the large day-trading study. The £100 figure is the requester's stop if the trial is clearly losing money after about a year; it is not a sized target. — [source: `intake.md`; requester, this session, 2026-10-29; Barber, Lee, Liu and Odean] (confidence: high for the sample-size gap and for the meaning of the £100 figure; medium for how damaging the weekly check is in practice)
- Practice mode, as documented, is a virtual-money Invest/CFD facility with a $50,000 play balance. It has not been shown here that fills include stamp duty, FX, and the real spread, or that a practice ISA exists. A paper result on those terms can look better than a later £50 ISA order. — [source: Trading 212 practice-mode help article; community staff reply, low confidence] (confidence: medium that the documented practice tool is a weak stand-in for a taxed £50 ISA trade)
