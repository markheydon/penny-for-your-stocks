# Problem Definition: Modest income from active share trading

- **Slug**: modest-trading-income
- **Created**: 2026-10-29
- **Inputs used**: intake.md | research.md

## Problem Statement

A UK-based individual with basic investing experience cannot tell whether active dealing in real shares — a small ring-fenced sum they can afford to lose, holds of a few days to about a week, checks at least weekly, and no leverage or derivatives — could support withdrawing about £100–£200 a month in the medium term. On an opening pot of about £50 that hope implies a rate of return far above long-run UK equity market results, published studies of frequent individual traders show results pulled below the market by costs, and there is no personal record yet against which to decide, over 12–18 months, whether the attempt is clearly just losing money and should stop.

## Affected Users & Stakeholders

- **Users**: The requester — a UK retail investor who already holds stocks and shares ISAs (ready-made plans and individual shares), describes themselves as a basic investor rather than an expert, and would be the person putting up the ring-fenced money, doing the weekly check, and living with a loss. No other user was identified. Demand in this repository is this one stated want, not observed trading behaviour.
- **Stakeholders**: The same person — they decide whether to start, whether any later money is added, and whether to stop. No funder, co-owner, or other decision-maker was recorded.

## Goals

- Reach an explicit stop-or-continue judgement after about 12 to 18 months, using the requester’s rule that the attempt stops if, after about a year, it is clearly just losing money.
- Find out whether withdrawing about £100–£200 a month is reachable at all. The requester accepts that this hope may never be met, and learning that is part of the point.
- Keep any money at risk inside a sum that can be lost. About £50 is an arbitrary start to test the waters, not a decision that the pot stays there. Further money is considered only if the short term is not already a clear failure, and the amount is not set now.
- Stay inside real shares in the UK, the United States, and Europe, excluding controversial areas such as weapons. A UK-only limit is a moral preference, not a hard limit. Penny shares are the starting interest, not a requirement if other shares fit the constraints better.

## Non-Goals

- Individual share recommendations or a get-rich-quick outcome.
- Leverage, contracts for difference, spread betting, borrowed money, or other heavily speculative strategies.
- Same-day dealing, or a routine that requires checking the market every day. The commitment is at least once a week.
- Choosing a broker, setting platform fees or platform limits, or giving tax advice. The requester placed platform fees, platform limits, and tax outside this assessment discussion. Cost figures already gathered stay as background on whether the income hope is realistic.
- Treating 12–18 months of one person’s trades as proof of a repeatable edge. The published persistence tests used vastly larger samples than this trial can produce.

## Success Metrics

- A recorded decision at about 12 months, and a review by about 18 months, on whether the trial is clearly just losing money and therefore stops. The £100 figure was a way of saying that, not a loss budget or a profit target. (baseline: no trial has been run; the repository has not tested a strategy; qualitative)
- Any capacity to withdraw cash, compared with the hope of £100–£200 a month. Arithmetic on a published long-run UK equity figure of 8.86% a year nominal (1899–2024, not a forecast): about £4.43 a year (~37 pence a month) on £50; about £13,540 of capital to support £100 a month and about £27,090 to support £200 a month without spending the pot. £100 a month is £1,200 a year, 24 times a £50 pot. (baseline: those figures; no personal return exists yet)
- Extra capital, if any, is added only after the short term is not already a clear failure, and the amount is chosen at that time. (baseline: unset)
- The requester can say, from the record, whether the income hope is still open or has failed, without reading a short run of results as a durable edge. (baseline: unknown; qualitative)

## Cost of Inaction

The existing stocks and shares ISA, used for ready-made plans and individual shares, continues, and the extra turnover of an active trial is avoided. The question the requester asked stays unanswered: whether a modest withdrawal is realistic, or whether the attempt is simply losing money. Left at about £50 and earning a long-run market rate around 9% a year nominal, the pot produces a few pounds a year, not £100–£200 a month. Background arithmetic on a fully invested £50 pot recycled every week is a large cost drag before spreads (about 26% of capital a year in stamp duty reserve tax on chargeable UK shares, or about 15.6% a year in foreign-exchange fees on overseas shares inside a sterling ISA). The project’s own goals already treat the chance of a good sum as close to zero. Doing nothing avoids spending a ring-fenced sum on that attempt. It also leaves no personal pass/fail record, so later money could be added, or the idea abandoned, without the 12–18 month test the requester asked for.
